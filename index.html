You run national television. Every year you decide how many of the twenty screens show your code: the look of your own milieu, shell chairs and lofts and primary-colour geometry. The rest show the common code that everybody understands: teak cabinets, sofas, family dinners.

Your salary follows your approval. Your bonus comes from every pink screen. If approval drops below one half, you are out. You have forty years.

How it works

Every household watches two of your shows a year, picked at random from your screens. Anyone can miss a show now and then, and a show in your code is lost on most people who can't read it. When people miss a show, they ask around. If someone got it, fine. If nobody did, they conclude it wasn't meant for them. If that happens with both their shows in a year, they hold a grudge. Grudges fade, but slowly. Over time some people learn your code: from friends who already read it, or at school.
